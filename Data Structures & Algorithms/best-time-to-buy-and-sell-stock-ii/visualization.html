Best Time to Buy and Sell Stock II

LeetCode 122 — Maximize profit with unlimited transactions

Array Greedy Dynamic Programming O(n) O(1) space

Problem Overview

Each day has a stock price. You may buy and/or sell, but you can hold at most one share at a time. You can buy and sell on the same day, and you may make as many transactions as you like. Return the maximum total profit (or 0 if no profit is possible).

Input: prices = [7,1,5,3,6,4] Output: 7 buy @ 1 (day 2) -> sell @ 5 (day 3): +4 buy @ 3 (day 4) -> sell @ 6 (day 5): +3 Input: prices = [1,2,3,4,5] Output: 4 Input: prices = [7,6,4,3,1] Output: 0
Key insight: a multi-day profit telescopes into the sum of its daily price changes. With no fees and unlimited trades, collect every upward step and ignore every downward or flat step.

Approach 1: Brute Force (DFS on Buy / Sell / Skip)

Intuition: Model every day as a choice. If you are holding a share, either sell it or keep holding. If you are free, either buy or skip. Take the best branch.

Time: O(2^n) Space: O(n)
Decision tree for dfs(day, holding) — two branches per day

This is correct but exponential. Memoizing (day, holding) collapses it to O(n) states — that is the DP approach below.

Approach 2: Greedy Daily Sum (Implemented)

Intuition: Whenever tomorrow is more expensive than today, "buy today and sell tomorrow" captures that rise. Linking all rises together reconstructs any longer profitable hold. Falls are skipped by selling first and buying back lower.

Approach 3: Peak-Valley Two Pointers

Intuition: Instead of inspecting every edge, jump from each local minimum (valley) to the next local maximum (peak). Each such pair is one transaction; their differences sum to the same profit.

Time: O(n) Space: O(1)
The same chart with valleys and peaks marked

Valley detection: advance while the next price is lower or equal. Peak detection: advance while the next price is higher or equal. Each index moves forward only.

Approach 4: Dynamic Programming (Hold / Cash)

Intuition: Keep the best profit for the two possible states after each day: hold (own one share) and cash (own none). Buying moves cash → hold; selling moves hold → cash.

Approach Comparison

Approach Time Space Pros Cons
Greedy Daily Sum (implemented) O(n) O(1) Shortest correct solution; one pass Only works with unlimited, fee-free trades
Peak-Valley O(n) O(1) Explicit buy/sell pairs More pointer bookkeeping and edge cases
Dynamic Programming O(n) O(1) Extends to fees, cooldowns, and trade limits More state than the greedy sum
Brute Force (DFS) O(2n) O(n) Explores every legal strategy Exponential; impractical beyond tiny inputs

Common Pitfalls

  • Confusing this with LeetCode 121. That problem allows only one transaction; this one allows unlimited transactions.
  • Adding negative changes. Accumulating every difference collapses to prices[n-1] - prices[0], which is wrong when prices fall.
  • Forgetting flat days. Equal prices produce a zero change; adding it is harmless, but treating it as a loss is not.
  • Forcing a gap between trades. Same-day sell-and-rebuy is allowed; no cooldown exists here.
  • Assuming one continuous hold is optimal. Selling before a dip and rebuying lower can add profit (for example, exiting at 5 before the drop to 3).
  • Returning a negative profit. Start at 0; doing nothing is always legal.
